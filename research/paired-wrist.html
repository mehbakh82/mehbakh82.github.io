Paired-view wrist radiographs

A patient-disjoint feasibility study and projection-level miss audit for pediatric wrist fracture detection.

Question

Would supervising which projection shows a fracture help a paired model beyond exam-level labels? I first asked whether a detector already produced plausible proposals for enough missed exams to justify training the fusion heads.

What I tested

I used a patient-disjoint development pilot, compared simple paired baselines, and audited the projection of matching proposals among missed positives. The best reported max-fusion result detected 215 of 230 pilot positives at 95.2% specificity.

Decision and follow-up

Nine distinct patients had localizable misses, one short of the prespecified minimum. The study stopped before training the fusion model and left the locked test unscored. All nine localizable misses had a matching lateral proposal. The frontal-only subgroup had just six positives, which limits interpretation.
